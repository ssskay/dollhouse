# Example photos: shot list

Every photo in `spaces/*/photos/` is AI-generated from the briefs below. None of them show a real person's stuff. They exist so the examples look like what you'd actually see when you open the door: messy, the way real storage is, because that's who this tool is for. In your own space, use stills from your own walk-through video instead.

The briefs were run with Codex's image generation, one session per space in parallel, with a screenshot of each space's 3D layout as a reference for what goes where. Codex saved them as PNGs; they're stored here as 1280 px JPGs to keep the repo small.

## Rules for every photo

- Photoreal, as if taken by an ordinary person with a phone in a hurry: landscape, slightly crooked, a little noisy, sometimes with phone-flash glare on plastic. Not staged, not styled, not a home-organizing ad.
- Light: whatever the space really has, which is usually not enough. Storage units: one caged ceiling bulb, light grey corrugated metal walls, scuffed concrete floor, the inside of a white roll-up door. Garage: bare drywall with tape seams, concrete floor with oil stains, one fluorescent tube light. Closet: white painted walls with scuffs, white wire shelving, only the hallway light.
- No people, hands or pets.
- No brand names, logos or readable product text anywhere. Generic shapes and plain colors.
- No characters, licensed toys, collectibles, figures, anime, or posters with recognizable art. Stuffed animals are plain (a brown teddy bear, a grey bunny). Framed art is plain abstract or landscape.
- The only readable text is the handwritten labels listed in each shot, in black marker on masking tape or on the cardboard, exactly as written. Everything else that would carry text (papers, file folders, book spines, shipping labels) is blurred, torn or blank.
- Match the layout: things on the left wall stay on the left, the back wall stays at the back. The reference screenshot shows where things are; never draw the screenshot itself, its colors or its labels.

## Mess: how real storage actually looks

The named things in each shot must still be visible and recognizable, because each photo illustrates one spot. Everything around them is a mess. Lean into it:

- **Nothing matches.** Bins in five different colors and sizes, some with cracked or missing lids, a lid from a different bin balanced on top. Moving boxes of every size, some reused from online orders with old shipping labels half peeled off (unreadable).
- **Everything is overfull.** Shelves sag. Totes bulge so the lids don't close. Things stacked on top of the shelf unit, wedged in sideways, hanging off the edge. Plastic grocery bags stuffed with more plastic bags.
- **The floor is where things go.** Stuff in front of the shelves, leaning on other stuff, a trash bag of clothes slumped against something, a rolled rug, a lamp with no shade, a laundry basket of random things, loose cords and bungees.
- **Labels are a mess too.** Old labels crossed out and rewritten, two layers of masking tape, one label peeling off, boxes with no label at all next to ones that have one.
- **Dust and wear.** Dusty tops, crushed box corners, taped box seams split open, a tote with a cracked side, a water ring on cardboard, cobwebs in a corner.
- **Random life stuff.** Things nobody would plan to store: a single dining chair, a stack of old magazines (no readable text), a bag of tangled cords, an empty plant pot, a crib mattress, a box of cables, a bike helmet.
- Untidy but believable. Not a hoarding show and not a disaster: an ordinary family that ran out of time to sort it.
